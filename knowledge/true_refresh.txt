Specific conversion boundaries established so far:

STAGE 81130 computes floating animation time at 72FF84 from its integer
counter 72FF80, calls 7F730, an optional callback at 72FF88, and 7FB90.
It conditionally passes the floating time to 80A60 at 81232, then increments
the integer counter at 81243. Movie/replay branches compute that time
differently. Increasing the integer counter at display frequency would speed
stage animation. Sampling a floating stage time is a separate operation from
advancing the original discrete counter and invoking its stateful callbacks.

BALL 8C330 loops over eight records with stride 1B0, using original status
and handler tables. It dispatches through 557470 at 8C406 and also performs
motion, transform, radius/rotation and countdown work. It calls 8B9D0/8AEA0
under flag guards at 8C49E/8C4C8 and decrements a record countdown at 8C71F.
Its mixed responsibilities preclude treating the whole routine as render-only
or multiplying every addition/decrement by a timestep. The ball array starts
at 738DF0; 739BB4 is its selected-ball pointer in this routine.

APE 9DAB0 copies each active ball position into its character object at +258,
then calls the character-specific callback at +5C or default 9D9A0. Character
state, animation advancement and pose sampling need a verified split; changing
stage time alone cannot make monkey animation or ball motion continuous.

CAMERA B74C0 dispatches through 56A098/56A090 and constructs view transforms.
WORLD, STOBJ, EFFECT, SPRITE and BACKGROUND have their own updates/callbacks;
their time-dependent arithmetic is not controlled by the frame-wrapper wait.
They have no original common timestep; conversion uses verified operation and
callback boundaries rather than altering every counter or arithmetic operation.

Queue/lifetime distinction: 1FD80 walks linked draw records, calls record[0]
with record[4] and a zero argument, and advances through record[8]. 6A1E0's
6A2AE/6A2B6 decrement consumes queued entries counted by 7196E8, which is
reset to zero at 6A2BE. The previous research incorrectly described that count
as an effect lifetime. Neither operation should be time-scaled as a timer.

Rates below 60 retain 60 scene/physics updates and present fewer fresh frames.

Rule/input/resource/save calls in 14F00, the logical counters at 15110/1511B,

Euler step at 60 Hz. Contact drag at 8B42D uses exponential time scaling;
